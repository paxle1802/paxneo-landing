WiFi Meter – Support

Measure your Wi-Fi signal right from your Mac's menu bar.

Need help or have feedback? Email support@paxneo.net. We usually reply within a few days.

Getting started

Once the app is open, your signal strength (dBm) appears in the menu bar. Click it to see noise, signal-to-noise ratio, link rate, channel and a 60-second chart. Click "Open details window" for a 5-minute chart, nearby networks, the speed test and settings.

How do I read dBm?

  • -50 dBm or stronger: excellent, good for everything.
  • -51 to -60 dBm: good, stable for HD video and video calls.
  • -61 to -67 dBm: fair, video calls may stutter.
  • -68 to -75 dBm: weak, likely to slow down or drop.
  • Below -75 dBm: very weak; move closer to the router or switch networks.

Finding weak spots at home

Open the Signal tab and walk around with your MacBook. The 5-minute chart shows where the signal drops.

Picking a less crowded channel

The Nearby networks tab lists networks around you and counts how many share each channel. Yours is highlighted. If it is crowded, change the channel in your router's settings.

Why are network names missing?

On newer versions of macOS, Apple only shows network names to apps allowed to use Location Services. Go to System Settings → Privacy & Security → Location Services and turn on WiFi Meter. The app never reads your location.

Weak-signal alerts

In the app's Settings, turn on alerts and pick a threshold (default -75 dBm). You are alerted when the signal stays below it for 10 seconds. If no alert appears, check System Settings → Notifications → WiFi Meter.

Speed test

The Speed tab measures download, upload and latency with macOS's built-in tool against Apple's servers, in about 20–30 seconds. The result is your real Internet speed, including both Wi-Fi and your provider's line.

Open at login and language

In the app's Settings, turn on "Open at login" to keep WiFi Meter in your menu bar. The app comes in 7 languages: English, French, Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Spanish and Vietnamese, following your Mac's language by default.

Removing the app

Quit the app with the Quit button in its menu bar panel, then drag WiFi Meter from Applications to the Trash.

Privacy

WiFi Meter collects no data. See the Privacy Policy.
